Rules for roasting

The cook should observe the following rules when roasting food:

(i)

The oven and other heat sources should be pre-heated to the right temperature before putting the food item. Preheating ensures even cooking and helps to achieve the desired texture and colour.

(ii)

Leave the roasted food for a few minutes after removing it from the oven. This period allows the juice to be redistributed, resulting in a more flavourful and tender dish.

Advantages of the roasting method of cooking

(i)

Roasting brings out the natural flavours of ingredients, making the food more tasty.

(ii)

Roasting creates a crispy and golden exterior on vegetables such as potatoes.

(iii)

Roasting requires little or no added fats or oils. The high heat in the oven allows the food to cook in its juices, reducing the need for added fats while keeping the dish moist and tasty.

(iv)

Oven roasting is a relatively simple cooking method. Once the food is seasoned and put in the oven, it requires minimal attention, allowing you to prepare other ingredients of the meal or attend to other tasks.

(v)

Roasting can be used for a wide variety of ingredients apart from vegetables such as meats, poultry, fruits, and even nuts. This versatility allows you to create a diverse range of dishes using the same cooking technique.

(vi)

When compared to frying or sautéing in oil, roasting is a healthier cooking method. It retains the nutrients of the ingredients, and the minimal use of added fats makes it a good choice for people aspiring to reduce calorie intake.

(vii)

Roasting allows you to create one-pan meals by combining different ingredients in a roasting pan. This simplifies the cooking process, reduces the number of dishes to clean, and allows flavours to blend together.

(viii)

Roasting pans and baking sheets are often easy to clean, especially if lined with parchment paper or foil. This minimizes post-cooking cleanup efforts.
