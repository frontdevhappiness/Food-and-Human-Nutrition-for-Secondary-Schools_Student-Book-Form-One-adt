Food groups

Introduction

A food group is a collection of foods that share similar nutritional properties. Foods are grouped together because they consist similar nutrients. For example, nutrients in cheese, milk and yoghurt include calcium and protein. In this chapter, you will learn about the concept of food groups, nutrients present in different foods and a balanced meal. The competencies developed will enable you to make proper food choices, prepare a balanced meal and retain the nutrients present in food during food preparation, cooking and serving.

The concept of food groups

Foods are divided into groups based on the types and nutrients they provide. Therefore, when planning meals, you should include a variety of foods from all food groups to prepare balanced meals. The main food groups include the following:

(a)

Cereals, starchy, roots, tubers, plantains and green bananas: Examples include wheat, millet, sorghum, rice, maize, Irish potatoes, cassava, yams and green bananas. They mainly supply carbohydrates for energy provision in the body.

(b)

Foods of animal origin: These include foods such as chicken, eggs, milk, fish, beef. These foods provide mainly protein for building and repairing cells and tissues.

(c)

Vegetables: This group include foods such as green leafy vegetables (amaranths, sweet potato leaves, pumpkin leaves, and cassava leaves), tomatoes, carrots, eggplants and sweet peppers. Vegetables mainly provide vitamins and minerals to strengthen the immune system.
