(ix)Roasting can be used to cook larger quantities of food at once, making it ideal for batch cooking or meals preparation for multiple servings.
(x)Roasted dishes including vegetables have an elegant and appetizing appearance, making them ideal for guests and special occasions.

Disadvantages of the roasting method of cooking

(i)
Roasting requires a longer cooking time; roasting is generally a slower cooking method than in the case with other techniques such as frying or grilling. Depending on the size and type of food being roasted, it may take more time to achieve the desired results.
(ii)
Achieving even cooking with roasting can be a challenge, particularly when roasting large or irregularly shaped pieces of vegetables. Using a roasting rack or turning the food during cooking can address this problem.
(iii)
Browning variability: While browning is one of the appealing aspects of roasting, it can also vary based on factors such as the food’s moisture content, sugar content, and proximity to the heat source. Ensuring consistent browning requires attention and adjustments during cooking.
(iv)
Roasting large quantities of food or multiple dishes simultaneously may require ample oven space. A limited oven capacity can be a disadvantage when preparing meals for a large number of people.
(v)Some delicate foods, such as soft or thinly sliced vegetables, may not be well-suited for roasting, as they can become overcooked or lose their shape.
