Food and Human Nutrition, and Chemistry: To understand the functions of various nutrients in our body, one needs to learn the elements contained in carbohydrates, proteins, and minerals. This involves the application of Chemistry. On the other hand, through Chemistry, one learns how to make bleaches and detergents which are used as cleaning agents in cleaning kitchen cloths and equipment.

Food and Human Nutrition, and Biology: In Biology people learn the role of nutrients in the body and the effects of their deficiency. They also learn about food preservation to prevent spoilage and diseases. Such knowledge is applied during food preparation and cooking in Food and Human Nutrition.

Food and Human Nutrition, and Economics: Economic factors such as food prices and income influence people’s food choices. Food budgeting is essential before preparing food. The application of knowledge from Economics is required in food budgeting, especially when planning meals because one should consider the amount of money available before purchasing food items. Knowledge of Economics enables an individual to understand that food preparation should always match with the money available. Economics is also useful in managing time, money and energy during planning, preparing, cooking and serving food, as well as when buying food from the market.

Food and Human Nutrition, and Agriculture: Food and Human Nutrition also relate to Agriculture since foods for human consumption are obtained from the farm. Growing foods requires an individual to know something about agriculture. Such foods involve fruits and vegetables, which are used in making beverages and appetizers. Likewise, producing animal-based food products, such as milk, fish, eggs and meat requires the knowledge of Agriculture.
