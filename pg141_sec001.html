(iii) Pressure cooking reduces the need for constant monitoring and stirring during cooking, allowing you to set it up and leave it until the cooking is complete.
(iv) Pressure cookers can be used for a wide range of dishes including beans, cowpeas and pigeon peas.
(v) The shorter cooking time and minimal liquid usage in a pressure cooker help retain more nutrients such as vitamins and minerals in the food compared to longer cooking methods that may cause nutrient loss.
(vi) Due to reduced energy usage and water retention, pressure cooker contributes to a lower overall environmental impact.

Disadvantages of pressure cooking

(i) High-quality pressure cookers can be more expensive than traditional cooking pots.
(ii) While modern pressure cookers have built-in safety features, there is still a risk of misuse, especially if the safety features are not understood or followed properly.
(iii) If the pressure cooker is not used correctly or if there is insufficient liquid, there is a risk of burning the food, which can be challenging to clean.
(iv)
Limited cooking space and the size of a pressure cooker can limit the amount of food that can be cooked at once. Cooking larger quantities of food may require multiple batches.
(v)
Difficulty in adjusting seasonings, once the lid is closed and pressure builds, causes difficult in adjusting seasonings or making changes to the dish. Seasoning adjustments are better made before cooking.
