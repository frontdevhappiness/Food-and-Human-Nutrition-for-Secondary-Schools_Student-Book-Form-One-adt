Nutritional value of fruits

The nutrient content of fruits varies considerably between varieties. Except for avocados and guavas, most fruits have no protein or fat. Their primary dietary contributions are carbohydrates, minerals, vitamins and water.

Carbohydrates: Fruits are an important part of a balanced meal as they provode energy without adding unnecessary calories. Unripe fruits contain carbohydrates in the form of starch, ripe fruits contain carbohydrates in the form of sugar.

Minerals: Ripe bananas, oranges, apricots and grapefruits are rich in potassium and magnesium. These nutrients are important in regulating blood pressure. Apples, guavas and passion fruits contain small amounts of sodium, which is important for maintaining a proper water balance in the body. Fruits such as strawberries and watermelons contain small amounts of iron, which enables blood formation. Oranges, blackcurrants and blackberries contain easily absorbable calcium, which strengthens bones and teeth.

Vitamins: Strawberries, oranges, lemons and grapefruits contain vitamin C, which is a powerful antioxidant. It helps in healing wounds and maintaining healthy skin. Papayas contain folic acid, which is an essential nutrient for a pregnant woman as it facilitates the brain formation of the foetus and prevents the occurrence of spina bifida.

Water: Watermelons, apricots, strawberries, grapefruits, blueberries, oranges, peaches, papayas, pineapples and plums contain large amounts of water of up to 90 per cent. The water present gives these fruits a refreshing quality.
