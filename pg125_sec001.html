Soup is a well-flavoured nutritious liquid served at the beginning of a meal. It is made from stocks of vegetables, meat, fish, grains and pulses. Soup is an important appetiser in the meal. It stimulates digestive juices. Most of the soups provide heat to the body, especially in the cold season.

Simmering cooking method

Simmering is a cooking technique that involves cooking food gently and slowly in a liquid at a temperature just below boiling point. When simmering, the liquid should be kept at a temperature where small bubbles occasionally rise to the surface and break gently. The exact temperature for simmering is around 180°F to 200°F (82°C to 93°C). Simmering is a common cooking method used for a variety of dishes, including soups, stews, sauces, and certain braised dishes. It allows for gentle and even cooking, ensuring that the flavours blend while preserving the texture of the ingredients.

When cooking by the simmering method, consider the following points:

  • (i)

    Cover the pot with a lid to retain moisture and heat, allowing the food to cook evenly and preventing excessive evaporation of the liquid.

  • (ii)

    Do not open the pot unless you are sure that the simmering is ready.

  • (iii)

    Keep the temperature at a point just below boiling. If the food is about to boil, add an extra broth or cooking liquid to cool it.

  • (iv)

    Use a pot that is large enough to hold all the ingredients comfortably without overcrowding. This allows the liquid to circulate the ingredients and ensures even cooking.

  • (v)

    Stir the food occasionally while simmering to prevent sticking or burning, especially for dishes with thick sauces or starchy components.

  • (vi)

    Simmering is a slow cooking method, so be patient and allow the ingredients to cook fully. The cooking time will vary depending on the dish and the ingredients used.

  • (vii)

    The liquid used for simmering, such as stock, broth, or sauce, is essential for infusing flavour into the food. Season the liquid with flavourings such as herbs and spices to enhance the overall taste and aroma of the dish.

  • (viii)

    When simmering is complete, leave the dish for a few minutes before serving. This allows the flavours to develop further and ensures that the food is at an ideal serving temperature.
