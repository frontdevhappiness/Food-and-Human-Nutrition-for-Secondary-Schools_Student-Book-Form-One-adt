(iii) Occupation of an individual

The meal prepared should meet the needs of the body according to the kind of work the individual performs. For example, sedentary workers require less energy-giving foods than manual workers.

(iv) State of the body or physiological status

This factor includes different conditions the body experiences such as disease, pregnancy, and lactation. These conditions affect food intake and nutrient metabolism. For example, sick people require more proteins and vitamins to aid the recovery process and strengthen their immune systems. Whereas type 2 diabetic patients do not need high sugary foods in their diets; pregnant women need more folate and iron in their meals to prevent birth defects and anaemia.

Time available: The choice of family food to be prepared will also be determined by the time available for shopping, preparing, cooking and serving food. For example, one can plan to prepare fresh beans instead of dry beans because the latter needs to be cooked for a long time. An individual with a gas or an electrical cooker can prepare many dishes in a short time. In contrast, the one using a charcoal stove may not do so, unless he or she uses multiple charcoal stoves at once.

Economic consideration: The budget allocated for family meals determines the type, quality and quantity of food to be purchased. For many families, foods such as beef, chicken and fish can be too expensive for their budgets. Hence, a family can buy alternative foods such as sardines or offal which contain similar nutrients.

The equipment available: One should consider the kitchenware and equipment available for preparing, cooking and serving foods as well as storage facilities.

Climatic condition: A meal should be prepared according to the prevailing weather conditions. For example, foods that are served hot such as soup and tea are preferable to keep the body warm in cold weather conditions.

Time of a day: Meals should be planned based on the appropriate time of the day, such as morning, afternoon, or evening. For example, supper should be light because a person is preparing to go to bed. Night time requires little energy since the body will be resting.

Religion, cultural values, traditional beliefs and taboos: Religion, cultural values, traditional beliefs and taboos determine what should be cooked and eaten. In planning for meals, one should consider these beliefs to prepare meals that are acceptable in the family or locality.
