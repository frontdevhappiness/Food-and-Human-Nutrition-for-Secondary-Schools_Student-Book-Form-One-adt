Carbohydrates: These are food substances that are composed of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen atoms. They produce most of the energy needed in the body; hence, they are referred to as energy-giving foods. Plants are the main source of carbohydrates. There are two forms of carbohydrates, namely starches and sugars.

(i)

Starches: These include foods from cereals, tubers and starchy fruits. Cereals include rice, wheat, sorghum and millet. Roots and tubers include Irish potatoes, yams and cassava, and starchy fruits such as green bananas.

(ii)

Sugars: These include foods such as honey, table sugar, and sugarcane.

Functions of carbohydrates in the body

The major functions of carbohydrates in the body are to

(i)

provide energy to the body for different activities;

(ii)

assist in lipid metabolism, a process of breaking down fats which is used to facilitate various body functions; and

(iii)

provide heat to the body. Carbohydrates are broken down to release energy, contributing to warmth.

Proteins: These consist of carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen and oxygen atoms. Nitrogen forms an important component of proteins because it is needed for making all cells in the body. Some common protein food sources include dairy products, legumes and nuts. Protein is essential for the growth of the body and repair of worn-out cells. The building blocks of protein are amino acids. There are two types of amino acids, which are essential amino acids and non-essential amino acids. Essential amino acids are obtained from the food we eat. Non-essential amino acids are synthesized in the body. Protein, which contains all essential amino acids, is called complete protein, also known as protein of a high biological value or first class protein. Complete protein is mostly found in animal food sources such as milk, meat, fish, poultry and eggs. A protein which does not contain all essential amino acids is called incomplete protein, also known as a low biological value protein or second-class protein. Incomplete protein is commonly found in plant food sources such as pulses and nuts.
