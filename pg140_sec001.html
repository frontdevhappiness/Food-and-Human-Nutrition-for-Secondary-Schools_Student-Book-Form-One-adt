Sealing ring: It is a round rubber fitted around the inner part of the cover. It forms a seal between the cover and the body of the cooker when one is cooking.

Overpressure regulator: This releases excess pressure from the cooker.

Cover handle: This is used to remove the lid from the cooker or to open the cooker.

Body handle: The handle is attached to the body of the cooker and fits underneath the cover handle.

Cover or lid: This locks over the body of the cooker. It is fitted with a sealing ring for proper closing.

Points to remember when using a pressure cooker

(i)

An adequate amount of water should be used to ensure that sufficient steam builds up inside the cooker.

(ii)

No more than two-thirds of the solid food capacity and no more than half of the liquid capacity should be in the cooker. If these amounts are exceeded, the control valve will fail.

(iii)

Ensure that the lid is correctly locked in place before starting the cooking process.

(iv)

Place the pressure gauge in position and allow pressure to build up to the desired level. A steady hissing sound indicates that pressure has built up.

(v)

Lower the heat to maintain the desired pressure and cook for the correct time. Timing should commence once the pressure has been reached.

(vi)

When cooking time is completed, turn off the heat and allow the pressure to fall either by leaving the cooker at room temperature or by pouring a gentle stream of cold water over it. Never attempt to open the lid or remove the pressure gauge before the pressure has come to normal.

Advantages of using a pressure cooker

(i)

It prepares meals very quickly. It is cost effective on fuel use, and it requires less time and energy to cook food, leading to lower energy consumption and reduced cooking costs.

(ii)

It is suitable for pulses with hard coats as they easily become tender during pressure cooking.
