Nutritive value of nuts and oil seeds

Nuts are good sources of fat, protein and fibre. They are also rich in vitamins such as folate, niacin, vitamin E and vitamin B6 as well as minerals such as magnesium, zinc, iron, calcium and sodium. Some nuts contain phosphorus and potassium. Walnuts are rich in unsaturated fats like omega-3 fatty acids, which improve brain and heart health.

Oil seeds, whether whole or processed, are also known for their nutritional value. They do not contain an appreciable amount of carbohydrates but contain a high level of B-vitamins. They add important nutritional values to the diet owing to the presence of protein, vegetable oil and fat-soluble vitamins like vitamin A.

Uses of nuts and oil seeds

Nuts can be eaten raw or cooked. They are also processed to make oil and other products such as peanut butter. They can be used as ingredients for baking foods such as biscuits and cakes. They improve food flavour and texture. They can be served with salads or used as snacks like roasted nuts. They are also used for garnishing and thickening sauces and stews. Oil seeds can also be fried or roasted and eaten directly.
