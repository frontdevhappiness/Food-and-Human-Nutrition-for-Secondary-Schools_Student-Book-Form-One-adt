Points to consider when preparing vegetables for cooking

Consider the following points when preparing vegetables for cooking.

(i)

Prepare vegetables just before cooking.

(ii)

To avoid nutrient loss, use fresh vegetables or immediately after removing them from the refrigerator.

(iii)

Wash the vegetables carefully in cold running water. Do not soak them to avoid losing water-soluble nutrients, including sugars and minerals.

(iv)

Vegetables which are to be eaten raw should be thoroughly washed with a mild clean and safe water.

(v)

Shred or cut vegetables after washing them to minimize the leaching of nutrients.

(vi)

Use a sharp knife to avoid damaging the cells containing vitamins.

(vii)

Do not cut vegetables into small pieces or expose them to air to prevent oxidation, which destroys vitamins B and C.

Note: Vegetable stock can be served directly or used in preparing sauces, soups, and gravies as it contains vitamins and minerals that have dissolved into the water during cooking.

Concept of a salad

A salad is a cold dish which is made by using a mixture of raw or cooked vegetables and fruits sometimes accompanied by meat or fish. It can be dressed with oil, vinegar, or other dressings. Salads make the meal attractive, stimulating and appetising. Since salads are made using vegetables and fruits, they provide essential nutrients to the body. The amount of nutrients in salads depends on the ingredients and the preparation method. It also depends on whether they are eaten raw or cooked. Cooking may reduce levels of some nutrients such as vitamin B and C. Salad can be served as a starter to a meal, an accompaniment to a main meal or as a filling to food. Salad ingredients include green vegetables such as lettuce, cabbage and spinach; other vegetables such as carrots, onions, celery, cauliflower and peas. Examples of salad dishes are fruit and vegetable salad, arranged vegetable salads and mixed cooked vegetable salads.
