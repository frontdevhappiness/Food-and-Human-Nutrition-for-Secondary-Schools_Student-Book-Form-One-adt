Note: It is important to note that having one or more of these factors does not necessarily mean someone will develop an eating disorder, but they may increase its risk.

Characteristics of people with eating disorders

Individuals with eating disorders often exhibit a range of physical, emotional and behavioural characteristics. It is important to note that each person experiencing an eating disorder is unique.

The following are some common characteristics observed in people with eating disorders:

Distorted perception of their body image: Some people with eating disorders often have a distorted perception of their body size and shape. For example, they may perceive themselves as overweight even when they are significantly underweight.

Restrictive eating patterns: In a condition like anorexia nervosa, individuals severely restrict their food intake, leading to significant weight loss and malnutrition. They may avoid certain food groups or severely limit their overall calorie intake.

Changes in eating habits: There may be a noticeable change in eating habits, such as eating alone, avoiding eating in public with others, or making excuses to avoid eating. People with binge eating disorder engage in excessive eating.

Social withdrawal: People with eating disorders may withdraw from social activities and isolate themselves from friends and family.

Emotional and behavioural changes: Mood swings, anxiety, depression, and feelings of guilt or shame are common emotional characteristics.

Development of physical signs: Physical signs of eating disorders can include rapid weight loss, dizziness, weakness, hair loss, dry skin, and changes in menstrual patterns (amenorrhea) among women.

Excessive exercise: Some individuals may engage in excessive exercise as a way of controlling weight and shape.
