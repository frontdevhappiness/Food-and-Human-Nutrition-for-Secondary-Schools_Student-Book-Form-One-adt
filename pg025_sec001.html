Food and Human Nutrition for Secondary Schools: Student’s Book Form One

Nutrient Function Source Nutrient deficiency condition and disorder
Sodium
(i) Maintaining the right acid-base (pH) balance
(ii) Proper functioning of nerves and muscles
(i) Animal sources: sea foods, salted meats and butter
(ii) Other sources: table salt
(i) Excess sodium leads to high blood pressure
(ii) Poor electrolyte balance in the body
(iii) Uncontrolled electrical disturbance in the brain
Iodine
(i) Regulates the thyroid gland to release thyroxin hormone
(ii) Essential for growth
(iii) Helps in brain and nervous system development and functioning
(iv) Helps in reproduction
(i) Animal sources: sea foods, butter and milk
(ii) Plant sources: foods grown in iodine-rich soil
(iii) Other sources: iodized table salt
(i) Mental retardation
(ii) Goitre
(iii) Dwarfism
(iv) Abortion
(v) Deafness
Fluoride For strong teeth and bones
(i) Animal sources: sea foods
(ii) Other sources: fluoridated water and toothpaste
(i) Excess amount leads to fluorosis or mottling of teeth
(ii) Tooth decay
(iii) Weak bones

Water: This is vital for life. It should be taken every day, especially in hot weather where large amounts of water are lost through sweating. About two-thirds of the body’s weight is water. The body requires water frequently for the following reasons:

  • (i) Forming an essential component in blood and body secretions such as digestive juices;
  • (ii) Carrying nutrients and oxygen to body cells;
  • (iii) Supporting the structure of body cells;
  • (iv) Assisting in food digestion, absorption and assimilation of nutrients;
  • (v) Assisting in the secretion and elimination of body wastes through the kidneys;
