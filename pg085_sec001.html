Effects of heat treatment on vegetables

Heat softens the fibre in vegetables, making them easily digestible. Heat also destroys harmful micro-organisms. Moreover, heat may change the vegetables’ texture, flavour, colour and nutrient contents. For example, heat coagulates protein in vegetables, making them hard, dry and indigestible. Heat can also destroy vitamins C and B in the vegetables.

Storage of vegetables

Like fruits, vegetables are also perishable. Therefore, use vegetables when they are fresh. When vegetables are not for immediately consumption, store them properly to avoid spoilage by micro-organisms. Keep leafy vegetables in a plastic bag before storing them in a cool place such as the refrigerator to avoid losing flavour, water and vitamins such as vitamin C. Alternatively, place leafy vegetables on a standing position in a basin of cool water and sprinkle water on them. Doing so will help them to stay fresh for some hours.

Mushrooms should be stored in a porous paper bag to increase their shelf life. They can also be kept in a refrigerator for some weeks. Note that vegetables such as fresh mushrooms and potato leaves should never be frozen. Flower vegetables, such as broccoli and cauliflower, can be placed in plastic material and stored in a cool place. Bulb vegetables such as onions and garlic can be stored in a cool, dry and well-ventilated place.
