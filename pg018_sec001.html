Functions of proteins in the body

Proteins perform the following functions in the body:

  • (i)

    Development of body tissues, maintenance or repair and replacement of worn-out tissues;

  • (ii) Formation of enzymes and hormones for regulating body processes;
  • (iii) Production of antibodies, which help the body to fight against diseases, and
  • (iv) Supplying the body with energy when a meal does not provide enough carbohydrates and fats.

Fats: These are food substances composed of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen atoms, like in carbohydrates but in different proportions. Fats are the most concentrated energy foods found in both animals and plants. Animal fat foods include poultry and dairy products such as butter, cheese, and fatty fish such as salmon. Vegetable oils include oils from sunflower and cotton seeds and palm and coconut.

Functions of fats in the body

  • (i) Fats act as an insulator. The layer of fat under the skin controls heat loss from the body to the environment, thus maintaining body temperature.
  • (ii) Fats protect internal organs, such as the heart and kidneys, from damage.
  • (iii) They provide a sense of fullness. Fats take longer to digest than carbohydrates and proteins do. Therefore, when included in a meal, fats can slow down digestion.
  • (iv) Fats are a concentrated source of energy. Foods rich in fat provide high energy value.
  • (v) They provide a reserve of energy for long-term storage, which can be utilised if the energy intake is reduced or stopped.
  • (vi) Fats improve the texture and flavour of foods.
  • (vii) They facilitate the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E and K) in the body.
