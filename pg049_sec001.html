If burns occur, cool the whole area with cold water for several minutes to reduce the pain and lower the risk of blistering. If blisters occur, do not break them. Apply some petroleum jelly and avoid exposure of the burnt area to the air as it will cause more pain. Minor burns can be covered with sterile gauze and bandages.

Scalds: These are caused by burning with hot liquid such as hot water or steam. For example, a hot steam which comes from food can also cause scalds. To prevent scalds, keep boiling pots stable and turn their handles away from the cooker. Use pot holders when removing pans or pots from the cooker. When opening lids while cooking, make sure that you direct the steam away from you. If scalds occur, deep the injured part in cold water. This will cool the body and, hence, reduce the pain. Do not break the blisters as they protect the injured part from bacterial infection. Apply petroleum jelly on the scalds.

Fire: Fire in the kitchen can result from a leakage of a gas pipe or from coming in contact with materials which can catch fire like curtains or kitchen cloths. Unintentionally turning on or leaving the cooker on may also cause a fire. To minimize this risk, always unplug electrical appliances or put off cooking stoves after use. Never pour water on a grease fire. Instead, use a large pan lid to prevent the fire from spreading. Covering the fire with a damp towel can also extinguish the fire. If you smell gas, turn off all gas jets and open the windows to disperse any built-up gas. Light the match first before turning on the gas when lighting the gas cooker. Do not store anything attractive to children near the cooker, such as snacks. Keep all items such as kitchen cloths and curtains away from the cooker. It is a good practice to have a fire extinguisher available for use but not for grease fire as it can spray or shoot the burning grease around the kitchen, thus spreading the fire. Keep children and pets away from cooking areas to minimize the risk of accidents that might lead to fire.

Cuts: You should do chopping or cutting of food items carefully to avoid cutting your hand and causing injuries. Do not put knives and sharp objects together with other utensils during washing. Broken glasses in the kitchen can also cause cuts. If cuts occur, clean the wound with a weak antiseptic solution or salt to prevent infection. Then, cover it with a sterile gauze. You can use plasters to cover minor cuts. Assess the seriousness of the wound; if necessary, consult the doctor. When using a knife, make sure that the sharp part is facing away from you. When handling sharp knives, hold them by their handles and keep the handles free from grease or oil to avoid slipping off. Keep all sharp and pointed utensils in a safe place. Avoid washing knives together with other utensils.
