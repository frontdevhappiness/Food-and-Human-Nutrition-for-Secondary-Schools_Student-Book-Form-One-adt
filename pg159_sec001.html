Eating disorders

An eating disorder is a serious health condition caused by avoidance or excessive consumption of food. It affects weight and body shape. This can lead to bad eating habits that may significantly impair physical health and normal functioning of the body. These disorders are more common among teenage girls and young women than among boys and men. If not managed effectively, eating disorders can result in long-term problems and, in some cases, can lead to death. The most common eating disorders are anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge-eating and pica.

Causes of eating disorders

Eating disorders can affect people of all ages, backgrounds and genders. However, eating disorders often occur during adolescence and may also develop during childhood or later in life. Some of the factors which may lead to the development of eating disorders have been identified, as follows:

(i)

Genetics and family history: Eating disorders are more likely to occur in people whose parents or siblings have an eating disorder.

(ii)

Gender: Eating disorders are more prevalent in females than in males because females are more conscious of their body images. However, it can affect individuals of any gender

(iii)

Age: Eating disorders often emerge during adolescence and early adulthood. The pressure to conform to societal beauty standards and navigate body image changes during these developmental stages can contribute to the risk.

(iv)

Body image dissatisfaction: Poor body image or dissatisfaction with one’s appearance can be a significant risk factor for developing an eating disorder.

(v)

Other mental health issues: Stress, anxiety, depression and other mental health issues can increase the likelihood of an eating disorder.

(vi)

A history of weight bullying: People who have been bullied for their weight are more likely to develop eating disorders. These include people who have been made to feel ashamed of their weight by peers, healthcare professionals, coaches or family members.

(vii)

Social media pressure: Exposure to unrealistic body images and unhealthy messages about dieting and appearance on social media can impact body image and contribute to the risk of eating disorders.
