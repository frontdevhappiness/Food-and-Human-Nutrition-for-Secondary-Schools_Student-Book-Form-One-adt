Stock and soups

Stock is a flavourful liquid that forms the base or foundation of many types of soups, sauces, stews, and other dishes. It enhances good taste and texture of the final dish. Stock is made from bones, meat, fish, or vegetables with aromatic ingredients in water. Some vegetables are not suitable for making stock. These are potatoes and green leafy vegetables. Potatoes cause sour taste stock, and green leafy vegetables cause bitter taste stock. The stock is made by the simmering method. This is a method where food is cooked gently and slowly. The slow simmering process extracts the flavours, nutrients, and gelatin from the ingredients, resulting in a rich and savoury liquid. The nutritional value of stock depends on the ingredients used. The following are points to consider when preparing stock for soups:

  • (a) Use clean and fresh ingredients for making stock.
  • (b) Cut fish or meat into small pieces to facilitate flavour extraction.
  • (c) Cover the ingredients with cold water and cook using a simmering method.
  • (d) To concentrate the flavour, remove the lid to let the water evaporate.
  • (e) Skim the stock to remove excess fat.
  • (f) Strain the stock when ready, cool quickly, and store it in a cool place.
  • (g) Use the stock immediately. You can refrigerate the stock for up to three days.
  • (h) Do not warm the stock more than once as this will create an ideal condition for bacterial growth.
  • (i) Cook meat stock for a longer duration than you do for vegetable stock to extract the maximum flavour, nutrients, and gelatin from the meat and bones.
