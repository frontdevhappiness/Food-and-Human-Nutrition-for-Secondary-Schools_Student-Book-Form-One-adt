(ii)

Sugar or syrup may or may not be added to fruit juice, depending on personal preferences. However, juice with added sugar adds to the levels of calories, resulting in weight gain. The choice of using either syrup or sugar in making juice is entirely up to personal preference and convenience. Some people prefer to use syrup since it mixes easily and quickly. Others may prefer granulated sugar when they want to have more control over the sweetness level. While sugar can certainly sweeten the juice effectively, it may require more effort to ensure that the sugar is fully dissolved and evenly distributed in the juice.

(iii)

When using more than one fruit type to make fruit juice, consider including the one with a sharp taste to enrich the flavour of the final drink.

(iv)

If the drink is to be served cold, chill it in a refrigerator or add ice-cubes just before serving.

(v)

Do not use metal containers when making or storing acidic juice as ascorbic acids from fruits react with metal from the container resulting in a metallic taste.

(vi)

Garnish the juice with a slice of the fruit used in making it or its plant’s part or any other flavourful leaves, such as mint leaves, before serving.
