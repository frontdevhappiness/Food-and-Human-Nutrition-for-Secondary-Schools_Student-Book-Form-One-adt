Exercise 4.1

Eating fruits either raw or cooked have various benefits in the body.

(i)Provide examples of fruits that can be eaten raw and those that can be cooked before eating.
(ii)Explain the advantage of eating raw fruits over cooked ones.

Uses of fruits

Fruits serve various purposes. They can be squeezed or blended to make fresh juice. Fresh and dried fruits may be used to make stew and fruit pies. They can also be used as an ingredient for making cakes such as a pineapple upside-down cake. Fruits can be used to make puddings and sponge fruit flans. Additionally, fruits are used to make jams and jellies by adding sweetness and preserving their flavours.

Points to consider when selecting fruits

To select safe and quality fruits, you should consider the following tips:

(i)

Choose ripe unbruised fruits. Avoid overripe fruits since they may contain harmful bacteria. Conversely, unripe fruits may be indigestible.

(ii)

The skin of the fruit should be smooth without blemishes. Citrus fruits must have fine skins.

(iii)

Check the fruit’s colour to find out whether it is ripe. For example, a ripe strawberry is deep red; a ripe banana is yellow; and a ripe orange is orange, as its name implies. A cut watermelon has a deep red colour; when selecting a whole watermelon, it should be firm. Ripe watermelons sound hollow when slapped.

(iv)

Dried fruits should look fresh and dry, without stalks. They should neither be shrivelled nor wrinkled.

(v)

Press the fruit to check its firmness. For instance, apples should be firm.

(vi)

Pay attention to the smell of the fruit. A ripe fruit’s smell is easily noticeable even without smelling it up close; for instance, a ripe pineapple has a sweet smell, especially near the stem.

(vii)

Check the stalk. Fruits such as pears should be a bit softer around the stalk than others.

(viii)

Look for the lustre. Certain fruits such as cherries and apples should have a waxy, shiny appearance.
