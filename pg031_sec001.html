Importance of a balanced meal

A balanced meal is very important to the body as it comprises of foods from all food groups that provide essential nutrients for growth, repair of damaged body tissues and strengthening the body’s immune system to prevent the body against diseases. It is important to eat a balanced meal at least three times a day to ensure adequate intake of all nutrients needed for normal functioning of the body. A balanced meal also helps in preventing depression and anxiety and improves thinking capacity. Likewise, a balanced meal promotes the growth and development of children and adolescents who are still in the growth stage. For elderly people, a balanced meal helps to maintain and repair worn-out tissues.

Factors to consider when planning balanced meals for family members

Meals should be planned for all family members to get appropriate foods with nutrients in the right proportions to meet dietary preferences and nutritional requirements. One should consider the following points when planning family meals:

Nutrient adequacy: It is important to ensure that the meal contains all the nutrients. This is achieved by including foods from all groups. Dietary fibre should also be considered during meal planning as it aids in digestion.

Food variety: Food variety is important to avoid food monotony and dietary deficiency in addition to making the dish appetising. This can be achieved by using various cooking methods and including foods with different flavours, textures, and colours.

Seasonal availability of food: It is wise to choose foods that are in season because they are fresh, nutritious, and cheap. Off-season foods tend to be expensive. Besides, most of them may lack freshness, because they have been processed or stored for a long time. Examples of seasonal foods are vegetables and fruits.

Individual body needs: This includes age, sex, occupation of an individual and the state of the body.

(i)

Age of an individual

The meal prepared should consider the age of each family member because nutritional requirements differ based on respective lifecycle stages such as children, adolescents, adults and the elderly. For example, children need more protein foods than adults do due to their rapid growth.

(ii)

Sex of an individual

The sex of an individual can influence the planning for meals. Women of Reproductive Age (WRA) require more iron-rich foods than men do to replace the blood lost during menstruation. Usually, adolescents and adult males require more energy than females do.
