• (ii)

    Avoid overcooking to retain shape, nutrients, colour and flavour.

  • (iii)

    Use the liquid obtained from boiling vegetables (stock) to make other foods such as soup and sauces.

  • (iv)

    Chop the vegetables into equal sizes to allow even cooking.

  • (v)

    Once the water reaches a boiling point, reduce the heat to a gentle simmer. Simmering ensures even cooking and prevents food from becoming too soft.

  • (vi)

    Start with cold water in the pot and bring the water to a boil before adding the food. When water boils it ensures even cooking.

Advantages of the boiling cooking method

The boiling method offers several advantages, making it a popular and practical cooking technique for various ingredients. The following are the advantages of the boiling method:

  • (i)

    The liquid remaining after boiling some foods can be used to prepare soup, sauces and gravies; therefore, the dissolved nutrients are not lost.

  • (ii)

    It is possible to boil several types of food together (such as pumpkin and potatoes). Hence, the process saves both fuel and time.

  • (iii)

    It is a simple and quick method of cooking, depending on the type of vegetable to be cooked.

  • (iv)

    It requires little attention.

  • (v)

    Boiled food retains its flavour.

Disadvantages of boiling

While the boiling method offers many advantages, it also has disadvantages, which should be considered:

  • (i)

    Boiling can cause water-soluble nutrients, such as B vitamins, vitamin C and minerals, to leach into the cooking water. When this water is discarded, the nutrients are lost, reducing the food’s overall nutritional value.

  • (ii)

    Boiling food, especially vegetables and pulses, in large amounts of water, can dilute the natural flavours. Their flavours may disperse into the cooking water, resulting in a less tasty end product.
