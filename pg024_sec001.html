Table 2.2: Minerals, their functions, sources and nutritional deficiency conditions or disorders

Nutrient Function Source Nutrient deficiency condition and disorder
Iron
  1. (i) Synthesis of haemoglobin that transports oxygen to the body cells
  2. (ii) Assists in the brain formation of a foetus
  3. (iii) Gives blood its red colour
  1. (i) Animal sources: liver, lean meat, egg yolk, poultry, fish and offal
  2. (ii) Plant sources: dark green vegetables, pulses, millet, sorghum, whole meal flour, bread, cocoa and peanuts
  3. (iii) Iron-fortified foods
  1. (i) Anaemia
  2. (ii) Fatigue
  3. (iii) Shortness of breath
  4. (iv) Dizziness and headache
  5. (v) Pale skin or pale colour of the inside of the lower eyelids
Calcium
  1. (i) For strong bones and teeth
  2. (ii) Assists in blood clotting
  3. (iii) Maintains a healthy nervous system
  4. (iv) Assists in the normal functioning of muscles and the heart
  5. (v) Immune defence
  1. (i) Animal sources: milk and milk products (cheese, yoghurt) meat, eggs and fish when you eat with bones
  2. (ii) Plant sources: green leafy vegetables, whole cereal grains and their products, nuts, oil seeds and legumes
  1. (i) Rickets in children
  2. (ii) Weak teeth
  3. (iii) Softening of bones in adults (osteomalacia)
  4. (iv) Delayed blood clotting
Phosphorus
  1. (i) Formation of bones and teeth
  2. (ii) Important in chemical reactions of proteins, fats and carbohydrates
  3. (iii) Maintaining the right acid-base (pH) balance
  1. (i) Animal sources: kidney, liver, eggs, fish, milk and milk products
  2. (ii) Plant sources: whole cereal grains and their products, legumes, nuts and seeds
  1. (i) Soft and deformed bones (rickets in children)
  2. (ii) Feeling of worry (anxiety)
  3. (iii) Stiffness of joints
  4. (iv) Fatigue
